Razorpay Buildathon · Track 03

What happens between a failed charge and a cancelled subscription

Razorpay retries a failed mandate three times on a fixed schedule and then halts it. Salvage is the decision layer in that gap: it works out why the charge failed, chooses what to do about it, and forces every money action through eleven compliance gates before anything reaches a customer.

Every gate cites the clause it enforces. Every refusal is written to an append-only, hash-chained audit trail. One file in the codebase is allowed to import the Razorpay SDK, and the build fails if a second one ever does.

The problem

Four blind attempts, all on the same clock

A mandate charge fails on the 28th. The customer's salary lands on the 1st. Razorpay's subscription retry does not know that, because it does not know anything about the failure — it fires at T+1, T+2 and T+3, same time of day, whether the card is dead or the account is three days from payday. After the third, the subscription moves to halted.

The lever is documented, not clever. Razorpay's own docs state that a manual charge attempt on an issued invoice does not count toward a subscription's remaining retries. So a precisely-timed attempt can be added on top of the safety net rather than spending it.

The pipeline

Diagnose with a model. Decide with code.

Six stages between the webhook and the money. The model appears in exactly one of them, and it does not get a vote on what happens next.

  1. 01EventHMAC verified on the raw body, then appended to a hash-chained log
  2. 02Normalise114 taxonomy rules → one of nine failure classes. No model, no I/O
  3. 03DiagnoseThe LLM. Returns a diagnosis and nothing else
  4. 04Policy11 rules, priority-ordered, first match wins. Each cites a source
  5. 05GatesAll eleven, in order, every time — no short circuit
  6. 06ExecuteIdempotency key and audit row written before dispatch
The LLM never chooses a money action.

Where the deterministic normaliser produced anything but UNKNOWN, the normaliser wins — and it almost always does. Across the whole evaluation the model drove 6 of 623 decisions. Its prompt context has no field for a name, a contact, an email or an amount, so leaking one is a type error rather than an oversight.

The gate chain

Eleven gates, in order, every time

Every proposed action is submitted to the whole chain before anything is dispatched. There is no flag, config value or argument that reorders or skips any of them, and G0_KILL is always first.

What makes it an agent and not a rules engine

A refusal is not the end of the decision

When a gate blocks something it can compensate for, Salvage substitutes the lawful action and submits that to all eleven gates from scratch. One decision, two full chains.

And the verdict does not travel with the action. When a deferred action finally comes due, the gate context is rebuilt from the database — the audit rows, the contact history, the opt-out state — and all eleven gates run again against the world as it is at that moment. A retry scheduled at a compliant hour that comes due at 19:32 is refused at 19:32. Compliance that is only checked at scheduling time is not compliance.

Results · 40 mandates, 3 seeds, 3 arms

What we claim, and what we don't

Net recovery

Not distinguishable

Salvage does not recover more money than three blind retries. A ±20% change to the simulator's calibration reverses the ranking, so no uplift is claimed anywhere.

Attempts per recovery

2.89–3.39

Against the baseline's 4.09–5.30 — comparable recovery for about 35% fewer attempts, on every seed.

Wasted on dead cards

0 vs 30 per run

The baseline retries hard declines three times each because it does not know what it is retrying. Salvage spends nothing on them.

0 vs 19–29 per run
